### Precise unresolved licensing question

LGPL 2.1 section 6's opening paragraph conditions distribution on terms permitting modification of the work for the customer's own use and reverse engineering to debug those modifications. Intel Developer Tools EULA August 2024 section 2.1.D(2) requires a prohibition on reverse engineering its executable Redistributables. The oneMKL October 2022 license separately prohibits reverse engineering and modification of the proprietary software or its operation. A component-specific permission in our draft cannot grant rights in Intel's proprietary implementation that Intel has not granted.

The remaining question is whether the tested shared-library separation and the draft's narrowly scoped permissions satisfy LGPL section 6 while retaining those Intel restrictions in this exact combined executable. The supplied agreements do not expressly resolve that scope interaction. This review does **not** assert that the combination is unlawful, nor does it assume that a blanket open-source exception overrides Intel's terms. Obtain a qualified compatibility determination for this exact arrangement or applicable rights-holder permission before marking the matrix clear. No message requesting vendor approval has been sent. Changing compilers/solvers is not authorized as a gratuitous workaround; an independently sourced permissive METIS variant would be a separate validated engineering option, not something to label completed here.
